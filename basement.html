Basement

Needs

The 3D concept adds a U-shaped basement stair beneath the existing hall staircase, entered from the hall under the upper arrival landing. Select Basement stairs for a cutaway that temporarily hides the surrounding shell and upstairs stair geometry; Whole floor restores them. The concept includes a real ground-floor opening, oak treads, light closed risers, dark guards, a turning landing, and a basement arrival landing; it does not model the basement rooms.

October 27 owner/builder decision: all four ground-floor recessed stair bookcases, shelves, books and carcasses are removed, replaced by a continuous painted enclosure to the existing lower-flight/landing soffits with flat base trim. No doors/storage are added. Photo-matched follow-up: omit only the basement descending entry flight's wall-side oak cap, eight black bars and three posts (world z=34.52 ft); retain its stringer, center-gap guard, both return-flight guards, retaining walls, treads and landings. The basement wall ends 5.58 inches below ground floor; the powder wall above is offset. Continuous entrance protection and any required graspable wall handrail need professional review, as do framing, structural support, underside protection and fire separation. A closed model surface is not a rated assembly.

Planning geometry only: the stacked stairs swap with the kids room and move to the west side, with the shared opening at x = 0.175-10.0083 ft, z = 26.775-34.6 ft. Turn the assembly 180 degrees: the turning platforms sit at the west wall and the hall entries/arrivals face east. Move the excavation and retaining walls with it; restore floor over the former central void. The entry flight retains approximately 46.9-inch-wide treads and the return flight 44 inches. Keep the 4 ft-deep turning platform across both flights. The provisional drop remains 10 ft, with eight 7.5-inch rises and seven 10-inch treads per flight. Turning and arrival elevations remain -5 / -10 ft. Confirm actual elevations, floor assembly, structure, headroom, handrails/guards, fire protection, and basement landing circulation with the architect. Basement rooms and walking between stories remain unmodeled; this is not a code-approved stair design.
