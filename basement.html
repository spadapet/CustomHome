Basement

Needs

The 3D concept adds a U-shaped basement stair beneath the existing hall staircase, entered from the hall under the upper arrival landing. Select Basement stairs for a cutaway that temporarily hides the surrounding shell and upstairs stair geometry; Whole floor restores them. The concept includes a real ground-floor opening, oak treads, light closed risers, dark guards, a turning landing, and a basement arrival landing; it does not model the basement rooms.

Planning geometry only: the wall-side entry flight extends to the adjacent wall face, with approximately 46.9-inch-wide treads. The return flight remains 44 inches wide. The opening, turning landing, and outer rails follow the widened edge; wall-side rails sit inboard of the wall. The provisional drop is now 10 ft, matching the 9 ft ceiling study plus an assumed 12-inch floor assembly. Each flight has eight 7.5-inch rises: seven 10-inch treads plus the landing rise. The turning landing is 5 ft below the ground floor, and the basement arrival is 10 ft below. Confirm the actual basement elevation and floor assembly before sizing the stair. An architect must resolve headroom beneath the upper flights and beside the recessed bookcases, structural framing, handrail continuity, guard openings/climbability, fire protection, and basement landing circulation. This is not a code-approved stair design.
