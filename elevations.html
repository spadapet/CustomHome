Latest post/entry correction: remove the decorative front-entry arch and curved gable notch, leaving a straight header with its underside 8 ft above the porch. Use 12 in-square white porch posts on 15 in brick bases; covered-patio timber posts are 9 in square on 12 in stone feet, shifted 1.8 in inward across the terrace to keep the wider feet fully supported. Roof shapes, cream brick, doors and lighting remain. These are visual proportions, not structural sizing; engineer posts, footings, headers and connections. This supersedes the curved surround and thinner-post descriptions below.

White windows all around: use white exterior window frames, sash and perimeter trim on every elevation, including continuous joined-window bank trim and sills. Retain cream queen brick, existing grilles, window geometry and white interior finishes. Earlier black side/rear window specifications are superseded; dark cameras, lights and door hardware are unchanged.

Selected brick format: cream queen. Use queen-size cream face brick with warm gray-beige mortar across the exterior. The model uses a nominal 8 × 3.125-inch face module including joints, approximately 5.76 bricks per square foot. Preliminary budgeting is about 29,400 bricks including 10% waste, not an order quantity. Confirm supplier dimensions, outdoor samples, final exposed areas, coursing and veneer support with the architect and mason.

Latest material trial: warm cream brick. Cream bricks with subtle warm-toned variation and warm gray-beige mortar cover the house on all four sides, including upper walls, gables, exposed wall bases and patio wall-head closures. This replaces the red-brown palette, not the brick geometry or course pattern. White siding and side/rear limestone remain superseded. Keep the preferred elevation's roof shapes, paired gridded front windows, light-wood doors, white trim/arch and white porch columns. Patio paving remains stone. There is no brick/siding toggle. Approve actual brick/mortar samples outdoors before selecting products.

This is a brick-veneer appearance study, not a structural masonry specification. Review support/footings, ties, lintels, cavity/insulation, flashing/weeps, movement joints and freeze-thaw durability. Brick can be durable and low-maintenance, but whole-life sustainability depends on sourcing, manufacture, wall assembly, maintenance and reuse; no environmental comparison has been performed. The front-window/interior mismatch and unassigned garage-upper volume described below remain unresolved.

Current elevation target: the preferred light-wood farmhouse pictured below replaces the optional-brick finish exercise. Use fixed red-brown brick bases, column feet and front steps; white siding/trim; paired narrow gridded windows; three tall vented gables; lighter weathered shingles; light-wood entry/garage doors; a decorative arched entry surround; and standing-seam porch/garage eyebrow canopies. The brick checkbox is removed.

Exterior study, not a coordinated floor plan: four ground-front windows, six main-upper windows and two garage-upper facade windows follow the picture. The taller garage shell is unassigned roof volume only, not a new room, floor, storage or access approval. Furnished interiors and saved layouts remain unchanged; front windows must be reconciled with room openings. Retain the current garage setback and 8 ft door heights. Roof connections, loads, garage fire separation, egress, glazing, height/setbacks and drainage need professional review. This supersedes the lower-garage-roof and earlier finish descriptions below.

See placement measurements and assumptions. Choose 3D exterior to orbit, or Whole floor / Upstairs to return to the furnished layouts.

The current direction adapts the English-country reference: warm cream limestone, medium-brown shingle gables, black exterior window trim, oak entry doors, and warm-cream garage doors. Simplify the front portico to one entry-column pair joined by a straight header beneath the small gable; remove the separate arch surround and its duplicated posts. Keep the outer porch columns, two inner-column lanterns, and garage lights. Interior window trim remains white. Earlier roof-color, arch, cream-window, and farmhouse treatments remain inspiration alternatives, not current geometry.

The shell follows the current stepped ground-floor outline, front porch, lower garage slab, and adapted upstairs envelope, rather than stretching the reference image into a rectangular house. The upper floor starts 10 ft above the house datum; main eaves are at 19 ft, with the tallest provisional ridge now at 29 ft. There is no room, upper-story facade, or window above the garage's front section. Retain the garage below with a lower single-story gable, eaves at 9 ft and nominal ridge at 15 ft. Close the remaining upper perimeter at the loft's south and bedroom's east walls. Roof overhangs extend beyond the nominal 55 ft 6 in wall width; the garage remains set back from the porch.

A darker taupe-brown stone band wraps the ground-level walls below the cream facade, with matching porch-column feet and contrasting stone step nosings. The covered kitchen/dining patio uses matching brown shingles. Cedar fencing and paver paths using the driveway as their crossing complete the site; camera and warm-light locations are shown separately. All additions are exterior studies; furnished layouts and window operation remain unchanged.

The sloping patio roof now has a raised wall-head closure, tapered side closures and a short upper-wall return, removing the visible gap above the 9-foot kitchen/prep facade. Pitch, cover limits and openings remain unchanged; flashing, loads, connections and structural approval are not established by these model closures.

Roof masses, intersections, stone thickness, exterior window detailing, porch columns/arch, garage-door styling, planting, and site grades are conceptual. The removed above-garage room/window supersedes the earlier exterior study. Coordinate the lower garage roof's junctions with the remaining upper walls, bearing/support, valleys and flashing, drainage, garage height, actual grade, setbacks, local height limits, egress, glazing, and all structural openings with the architect.
