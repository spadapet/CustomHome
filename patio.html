Patio

Covered kitchen/dining patio study

Match the patio terrace and exposed slab edges to the taupe-brown stone wrapping the base of the house, using the same stone color and texture. Keep the darker steps, contrasting nosings, and warm step markers distinct; surrounding garden paths retain their existing paver finish.

View the covered patio. Limit the outdoor space to the recess beside dining and across the full kitchen/prep frontage: 29 ft long × 9 ft 1.2 in deep, x = 26.5-55.5, z = -2.1-7 ft. The rear edge aligns with the dining-room rear wall line, and the far end stops at the prep-room end wall. No terrace, support foot, furniture, or garden step may extend beyond this envelope; the roof alone continues houseward as described below. This supersedes the deeper 18 × 16 ft study.

Use one lower brown-shingle roof, not two overlapping roofs: its center is y = 9.5 ft with a 3/16 slope. The single plane spans x = 26.5-55.5, z = -2.1-10 ft, including thickness; only its house-side portion extends past z = 7 to cover the kitchen/prep extension. It does not enlarge the outdoor patio or project beyond the dining rear/prep end lines. The former upper canopy and separate prep lean-to are consolidated into this lower cover. Six timber posts and two ceiling lights meet its underside. Paving at house level remains inset 3 inches from the adjoining dining/kitchen walls and cut around the stair pocket; its overall bounds are x = 26.75-55.5, z = -2.1-6.75 ft. Keep the existing inward dining door and stacked casements; leave the modeled 4 ft-deep × 3 ft-wide door approach clear.

Roof connection correction: the slope intentionally rises toward the house, but the model had an unclosed gap above the 9-foot kitchen/prep wall. Add a raised headwall to the roof underside, tapered side closures above the existing wall heads, and the short upper-wall return at x = 54-55.5 ft. The roof no longer appears to float above that wall; its pitch, cover footprint, post/beam alignment and door/window sizes stay unchanged. These are conceptual enclosure corrections, not engineered supports, flashing or a roofing specification.

Rotate the 5 × 3 ft outdoor table to suit the shallower patio, centered (37.5, 3.45), with four chairs. Move the three garden steps 24 inches left when viewed from the garden facing the house, from center x = 49 to x = 51 ft. Their widest footprint is now x = 47.6-54.4, with z = -2.1-1.2 unchanged; they descend north without projecting beyond the dining rear line. Resize the terrace returns around the shifted pocket without moving the patio boundary, roof, posts, table, or fixtures. A short uncovered paver connector follows the step center. Furniture circulation remains a static study, not occupied-use approval.

The roof, terrace, and step rear edges remain approximately 30.23 ft from the rear boundary; the house rear-wall face remains 30 ft away. The separate garden walkway runs along the rear fence at z = -31.075 to -27.575 ft, not beside the house. The short 3 ft 6 in-wide spur now meets the shifted step foot at x = 49.25-56.645, z = -5.6 to -2.1. The former long connector from the rear fence is lawn; the spur is not a terrace extension. Confirm survey, setbacks/coverage, structure, loads, drainage/flashing, threshold waterproofing, grades, guards/handrails, and a step-free alternative. This is not permit-ready or an engineered roof design.

Rear-yard firepit: cold storage only

Do not operate the firepit under the patio roof. The Birmingham Fire Department requires 15 feet from structures and combustible materials for approved portable fireplaces, in accordance with manufacturer instructions; open recreational fires require 25 feet. The approximately 30-foot-deep rear yard cannot provide 15 feet on both sides of the modeled 30-inch unit between the cover and cedar fence. A screened or smokeless design does not waive these clearances.

Both front-yard proposals are removed. The firepit is back in the rear yard at (40, -15) ft, closed and unlit on a 4-foot-square pad, outside the patio cover and away from the paths. This is fully extinguished, cold storage only, not a burn area: the placement does not meet the checked operating separation between the house/patio and cedar fence. No approved rear burn location has been established. Confirm the actual unit and site with the Fire Department before any use; changing the model location does not waive the clearance rules.

Requested firewood storage beside the bins

The wood rack now runs along the garage wall immediately north of the waste-bin pad, centered (56.65, 22) ft: 8 feet long, 16 inches deep, nominal 4-foot stack height, raised 6 inches, with a top cover and open sides. Allow logs no longer than 16 inches; verify actual logs and rack products. The cover spans z = 17.9-26.1, stopping before the existing bin pad at z = 27.4. A flush infill pad supports the wall-side feet; outer feet use the existing pavers, without stacking overlapping slabs.

The rack leaves approximately 33 inches of existing paving, and 40 inches to the fence rail, locally. It does not create a narrower pinch point than the stored carts, whose passage is already restricted. Gate/door locations and the carts stay unchanged. These are static closed-object checks, not accessible-route or cart-lid/rolling approval.

This is the requested storage position, not fire-safety approval. The close house separation does not satisfy the preferred 30-foot woodpile separation; review fire, pest and moisture risks and local storage requirements before construction. Confirm rack load/anchorage, drainage, real cart lids, log dimensions and maneuvering. Neither firewood nor firepit remains in the front yard.
