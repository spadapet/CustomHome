Home vision / Front entry

Front Entry

Keep the interior entry wall clear: no built-in console cabinet or wall picture above it.

Guest coat closet and powder-room alcove

Provide a separate guest coat closet beside the powder room, with both doors opening off a hall-facing alcove. Closet bounds are x = 7.1-12.2 ft, z = 35.1-37.5 ft: nominally 5.1 ft wide and 2.4 ft deep, approximately 24.6 inches clear depth between finished walls. Include a continuous hanging rail, upper shelf, and two closed white-oak Shaker doors with satin-gold pulls in a 48-inch opening. This replaces the former small powder-area linen cabinet; guests do not enter the bathroom to store coats.

The alcove is open to the entry hall at its east edge, x = 12.2 ft, z = 37.5-41 ft. It has approximately 37.8 inches clear depth between walls; the powder door swings inward rather than into this space. Use the 1-foot strip previously added to the office, returning the office to 12.2 × 13.2 ft. Keep stairs, French doors, and exterior footprint fixed. Verify hanging capacity, door operation, occupied circulation, and accessibility with the architect. See the guest-entry view and powder-room details.

Exterior steps and stone contrast

Simplified entry supports: remove the separate full-height stone arch surround that doubled the posts. Use one aligned pair of limestone entry columns at x = 11.9 / 19.3 ft, z = 60.5 ft, joined by a simple cream header under the small gable. The clear opening between shafts is approximately 6 ft 9 in, and the header underside is 8 ft high. Keep one stone foot per column, two lanterns on this pair, and the two outer porch columns for the long canopy. Entry doors, steps, porch slab, mulch, and plants stay unchanged. Column sizes, beam spans, bearing, footings, and roof junctions remain an architectural/structural study, not an engineered support design.

The front elevation study uses taupe-brown base stone below the cream facade, matching porch-column feet, and darker stone steps with distinct dark nosings rather than bright white treads. Three provisional 5.4 in rises and 13.2 in goings descend from the porch to the paver walk; recessed warm markers illuminate the risers without a fixture in front of the door. Keep the lanterns on the porch pillars. Confirm slip-resistant samples, actual grade, consistent stair dimensions, handrails, and accessible entry with the architect.

Entry approach: use the garage/driveway and the remaining porch-side paver connection to reach the steps. Remove the separate straight walkway from the porch crossing toward the street/front lot boundary; leave lawn there. The driveway stays continuous without a separate paver apron across it.

Front-door smart lock

Use a Wyze smart lock on the active leaf of the double front entry. Exact model, finish, active-leaf handing, and exterior keypad/reader arrangement are not selected. The 3D model shows one schematic interior lock body above the existing pull; it is not a product rendering or an approved installation.

Before ordering or drilling, confirm the selected Wyze model with the door supplier: door thickness, bore/backset, available stile width beside the glazing, deadbolt/latch compatibility, pull spacing, weather exposure, battery access, and free interior egress. Coordinate the inactive leaf's astragal, strike, and flush bolts. Do not assume compatibility with a multipoint door system or that every Wyze model uses the same exterior hardware.

Wants

  1. Love the stairs that lead to the front porch. It’s wider and safer too.

  2. Front door is a double door entry, without surrounding sidelights or a transom. Retain glazing within the door leaves. No entry rug or runner; leave the oak floor exposed.
