Electrical

Outdoor camera and lighting placement

Open Cameras + lighting. These are candidate locations chosen around entrances and circulation, not verified surveillance coverage. Arrows show intended aim, not lens width or recording distance.

IDLocation and purpose
C1Doorbell-height camera beside the front door, about 4.2 ft above the porch, for visitor faces. It is separate from the pillar lanterns.
C2Above the garage doors, aimed down the private driveway for arrivals and vehicles; avoid the garage lanterns in the lens.
C3Revised house-side patio post at (41, 8.5, 6.05) ft, looking across covered seating/dining access rather than through glazing. Moves with the constrained cover.
C4Revised rear patio post at (27.1, 7.8, -1.75) ft, looking toward the rear lawn/fence. No camera remains at the former projected patio edge.
C5West rear house corner, looking along the side path toward the relocated gate at x = -10.605 ft, z = 45 ft, behind the first front-side window. The gate follows the fence-edge path after the eastward house placement. The west access downlight also aims toward this gate; both fixture positions stay unchanged. Recheck actual coverage across the wider west side yard.
C6East house corner, looking along the garage-side path toward the gate immediately behind the side garage door. Its aim and the side-entry downlight follow the relocated gate.

Except C1, mounts are roughly 9-10 ft above the illustrative garden grade. Home-run exterior-rated CAT6/PoE to the NVR hub; confirm the selected doorbell's PoE/transformer requirements separately. Detail weather-rated boxes, drip loops, surge protection, and service access. Use narrow views/privacy masks on the side paths and exclude neighboring windows/yards and unnecessary public areas. Do not record through glass. Final lenses, obstructions, face identification, IR reflections, and nighttime glare require on-site testing.

Remove all eleven short path-post/bollard lights throughout the property, including their gold lighting-plan markers. Do not relocate or replace them. Keep the two warm porch-pillar lanterns and garage pair, two shielded patio ceiling lights, one downward driveway light, one at each side approach, and recessed markers on all six front/patio steps. Target warm 2700 K, low-glare downward light: dimmable patio and motion-boost driveway/side approaches. Separate lights from camera lenses and aim within the property, not at neighbors or the street.

Brightness is illustrative, not a photometric plan. Verify wet-location ratings, glare/spill, fixture spacing, voltage/transformer sizing, GFCI protection, controls, and required lighting with the electrician.

Keep the patio ceiling lights and camera locations unchanged. The main walkway follows the rear/side fences, but all path-post lights are omitted, including the rear, fence-side, and front fixtures. Verify adequate illumination along the lawn-crossing patio connector and gate turns without these posts; this model does not establish photometric coverage. Terrace/steps keep their outdoor limits; the roof continues houseward over the kitchen/prep extension.

Needs

  1. Whole house CAT6 wiring. Ethernet in: offices, by TVs in living and master.

  2. Ethernet and plugs for TVs should be hidden behind the TV

  3. Multiple Ethernet outlets in office

  4. Car charging plugs in garage

  5. Media closet/hub for all CAT6 wires, fiber, coax (TV antenna), security camera NVR

  6. Wire CAT6 for external and internal POE security cameras.

  7. One high-mounted electrical outlet in the master bedroom, hidden behind the TV

  8. Electrical outlets on the front porch for Christmas lights and other decorations

  9. Outdoor electrical outlets positioned for hanging and seasonal decorations

Wants

  1. Wire for alarm system

  2. TV antenna on roof

  3. At least one electrical outlet in each walk-in closet

  4. Four-outlet receptacles in the bedrooms to reduce the need for extension cords
